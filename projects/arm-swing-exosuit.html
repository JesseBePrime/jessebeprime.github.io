Undergraduate honors thesis

Arm-swing exosuit for pathological gait

Initial design and preliminary testing of a cable-driven soft exosuit intended to assist arm swing without adding a rigid structure around the upper body.

Role
Initial concept, mechanical design, and prototype testing
Context
Undergraduate honors thesis
Tools
SolidWorks and preliminary testing with a healthy participant
Design motivation

Assisting arm motion while keeping the device soft and wearable

Stroke and spinal cord injuries can reduce arm swing during walking. This project explored whether a cable-driven wearable device could apply assistive forces while avoiding a rigid arm mechanism that would restrict the user.

The work focused on creating an initial platform for future research rather than claiming a clinical outcome. The prototype established a wearable architecture and supported preliminary testing with a healthy subject.

Prototype architecture

A waist-mounted actuator routes cable force to the upper body

The system places the motor-driven pulley and electronics at the waist. This keeps the heavier components close to the torso while the wearable harness and cable path transmit assistance to the arm.

Contribution and handoff

The thesis established the first prototype for continued development

I completed the initial mechanical design, assembled the wearable prototype, and conducted preliminary testing with a healthy participant. After the thesis, I transferred the project to a graduate student for continued development.

The project introduced me to the practical constraints of human-device interaction, including component placement, cable routing, wearer comfort, and the need to separate promising prototype behavior from validated clinical benefit.
