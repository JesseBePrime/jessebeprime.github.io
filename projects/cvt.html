Graduate research

Actively controlled V-belt CVT for wearable robotics

A compact robotic transmission that changes ratio during operation and adds integrated braking and clutching modes for a wider range of actuator behavior.

Role
Mechanical and mechatronic system design
Context
M.S. research, HGN Lab for Bionic Engineering
Tools
MATLAB, SolidWorks, Fusion, C, Simulink, LabVIEW
Challenge

One actuator must operate across changing torque and motion demands

Wearable and humanoid robots encounter tasks that place different demands on actuator torque, speed, and output impedance. A fixed transmission forces the drive system to operate around a single mechanical compromise.

My research adapts the V-belt continuously variable transmission used in larger machines to the scale and packaging constraints of a robotic joint. The transmission changes ratio by varying the effective diameter of its pulleys.

Mechanical design

Variable ratio, clutching, and static holding in one mechanism

A dedicated actuation motor changes the pulley geometry to shift the transmission ratio. At one end of travel, the mechanism releases belt compression so the output can move with low impedance. At the other end, a friction element engages the pulley to hold the output without continuous motor torque.

  • Miniaturized V-belt pulleys for a robotic actuator envelope
  • Motor-driven ratio adjustment through a compact linear mechanism
  • Integrated clutch mode for low-impedance output motion
  • Integrated brake mode for static position holding
Mechatronics and control

Sensing and control connect commanded ratio to pulley position

The test platform combines motor drivers, encoders, Hall sensors, linear potentiometers, PIC32 microcontrollers, a Raspberry Pi, and a LabVIEW interface. A closed-loop controller commands the CVT position while feedback tracks motor velocity and the linear position of the shifting mechanism.

Prototype integration

The completed platform supports mechanism and actuator-level testing

I carried the system from transmission modeling and CAD through manufacturing, sensor integration, embedded control, and benchtop testing. The resulting platform combines the variable transmission with the actuator hardware and control electronics needed to study its behavior under controlled conditions.

Experimental validation

Testing separates transmission capability from actuator response

The experimental program evaluates the transmission across its operating configurations and characterizes how quickly it can move between commanded ratios.

Transmission ratio Measured the available torque-ratio range across pulley positions.
Brake behavior Verified that the integrated brake can lock the actuator output.
Output impedance Compared high-ratio, low-ratio, and clutched operating configurations.
Ratio-change response Characterized rise time as a function of command size and belt velocity.
